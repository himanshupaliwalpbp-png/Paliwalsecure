## Quick Answer

Sole proprietors have unlimited personal liability — business debts are personal debts. Need: health insurance (₹5,000-10,000/year), term life (₹3,000-8,000/year — family protection), public liability (₹5,000-15,000/year), professional indemnity (₹5,000-15,000/year), fire & burglary (₹3,000-10,000/year). Total: ₹21,000-58,000/year. One customer lawsuit can cost ₹5-25L.

## FAQ

### Q: Is sole proprietor personally liable for business debts?
**A:** Yes. Unlike Pvt Ltd (limited liability), sole proprietors have UNLIMITED personal liability. Business debts, lawsuits, and claims can be recovered from personal assets (home, savings, investments). Insurance is critical — it is the only protection between business risk and personal assets.

### Q: What insurance should a sole proprietor buy first?
**A:** 1) Health insurance (₹5,000-10,000/year — personal medical cover), 2) Term life (₹3,000-8,000/year — family protection if you die), 3) Public liability (₹5,000-15,000/year — customer/visitor injury). These three are non-negotiable. Add PI + fire as business grows.

### Q: Can sole proprietors claim insurance as business expense?
**A:** Yes. Business insurance (PL, PI, fire, burglary) deductible under Section 37(1). Health and life insurance for the proprietor qualify for 80D/80C (old regime). The business and personal insurance are treated separately for tax purposes.

### Q: Does sole proprietor need professional indemnity?
**A:** Yes, if you provide professional services (consulting, design, accounting, legal). PI covers claims of negligence, errors, or bad advice. One dissatisfied client lawsuit can cost ₹2-10L. Premium: ₹5,000-15,000/year for ₹10L cover.

### Q: What is the difference between sole proprietor and partnership insurance?
**A:** Sole proprietor: insurance is in YOUR name (unlimited liability). Partnership: key partner insurance is needed (firm as nominee). Both need PL + PI + fire. Sole proprietor has higher personal risk — insurance is the only liability shield available.
