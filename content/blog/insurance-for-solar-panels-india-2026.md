## Quick Answer

Solar panel insurance covers: damage to panels (fire, hail, wind, theft), third-party liability (panel falls on person/property), and loss of income (if panels stop generating revenue). Premium: ₹5,000-15,000/year for ₹10L panel value. Some home insurance policies include solar panels — verify coverage. MNRE recommends insurance for all solar installations.

## FAQ

### Q: Are solar panels covered under home insurance?
**A:** Sometimes. Some home insurance policies include rooftop solar as "fixtures" (covered under structure). Others exclude them or require separate add-on. Verify in your policy wording. If not covered, buy solar panel insurance separately.

### Q: What does solar panel insurance cover?
**A:** 1) Physical damage: fire, hail, windstorm, lightning, theft, vandalism, 2) Third-party liability: panel falls and injures someone or damages property, 3) Loss of income: if panels generate revenue (net metering) and are damaged, covers lost income during repair. Premium: 0.5-1% of panel value per year.

### Q: Is solar panel insurance mandatory?
**A:** Not legally mandatory, but MNRE (Ministry of New and Renewable Energy) recommends it. If you have a solar loan, the lender may require insurance. For commercial solar installations (above 10kW): insurance is strongly recommended given the investment size.

### Q: Can I claim solar panel insurance as tax deduction?
**A:** If solar panels are for personal residence: no tax deduction. If for business (commercial installation): premium is deductible under Section 37(1). Also claim accelerated depreciation (40% per year) on solar panels under Section 32. Consult your CA.

### Q: What is loss of income cover for solar panels?
**A:** If you sell excess power to the grid (net metering), damaged panels mean lost revenue. Loss of income cover pays the revenue you would have earned during the repair period. Essential for commercial installations. Premium addition: ₹1,000-3,000/year.
