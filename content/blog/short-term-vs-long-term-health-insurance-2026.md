## Quick Answer

Short-term health insurance covers 1-3 years and offers flexibility to switch insurers. Long-term (3-year) policies offer premium discounts (10-15%) and protection against premium hikes but lock you in. For most families, long-term is better — it saves money and ensures continuous coverage. Short-term suits those expecting life changes (job switch, relocation).

**At a glance:**

| Parameter | Short-term (1 year) | Long-term (3 years) |
|---|---|---|
| Policy tenure | 1 year (renewable) | 3 years (one-time payment) |
| Premium discount | None | 10-15% (vs annual) |
| Premium hike protection | No (hike at renewal) | Yes (locked for 3 years) |
| Flexibility to switch | High (switch at renewal) | Low (locked for 3 years) |
| Portability | Easy (annual porting) | Complex (mid-term porting) |
| Sum insured reset | Annual reset | Annual reset (same as short-term) |
| Tax benefit (80D) | Annual claim | One-time (full 3-year premium) |
| Best for | Expecting life changes | Stable, want savings |

## Exclusions Comparison

Both Short-term (1 year) and Long-term (3 years) have standard health insurance exclusions (per IRDAI guidelines):
- Pre-existing conditions (during waiting period)
- Cosmetic/plastic surgery (unless accident-related)
- Dental treatment (unless due to accident)
- Self-inflicted injuries
- War, nuclear events, terrorism

**Plan-specific exclusions vary.** Always read the policy wording.

## Claim Process

### Cashless Claim (if applicable)
1. Inform insurer within 48 hours
2. Visit network hospital
3. Hospital sends pre-authorization
4. Insurer approves within 4-24 hours
5. Treatment proceeds (no upfront payment)

### Reimbursement Claim
1. Inform insurer within 48 hours (planned) / 7 days (emergency)
2. Get treated at any hospital
3. Submit documents within 15-30 days
4. Insurer reimburses within 7-15 working days

## Common Mistakes

1. **Choosing based on premium alone** — lower premium may mean lower coverage
2. **Not reading exclusions** — assuming everything is covered
3. **Ignoring waiting periods** — assuming day-one coverage for all conditions
4. **Not comparing network hospitals** — assuming your preferred hospital is covered
5. **Not declaring pre-existing conditions** — claim rejection risk
