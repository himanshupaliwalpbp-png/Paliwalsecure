## Quick Answer

NGOs and trusts need: (1) Public liability (₹10,000-30,000/year), (2) Workers' compensation (₹5,000-15,000/year), (3) Asset insurance (office equipment, vehicles — ₹5,000-20,000/year), (4) Directors' and Officers' liability (₹10,000-30,000/year), (5) Cyber insurance (beneficiary data — ₹10,000-25,000/year). NGOs handling foreign funds must maintain FCRA compliance.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Buying insurance only after a loss** — insurance is prevention, not recovery
2. **Under-insuring** — buying minimum cover to save ₹2,000/year premium
3. **Not declaring pre-existing conditions** — claim rejection + policy void
4. **Choosing lowest premium only** — lower CSR = claim struggle
5. **Not reading exclusions** — assuming everything is covered
6. **Not comparing insurers** — buying from first quote received
7. **Letting policy lapse** — gap in coverage = no protection
8. **Not updating nominee/address** — claim complications
9. **Buying investment-linked insurance** — term + mutual funds is better
10. **Not consulting a certified advisor** — DIY insurance can be costly

## FAQ

### Q: Do NGOs need public liability insurance?
**A:** Yes. NGOs work with beneficiaries, volunteers, and communities. If a beneficiary or volunteer is injured during NGO activities (camp, distribution, workshop), the NGO is liable. PL covers medical costs + legal defense + compensation.

### Q: Is D&O insurance needed for NGO trustees?
**A:** Yes. Trustees and board members can be personally liable for decisions that cause financial loss to the NGO or third parties. D&O insurance protects personal assets of trustees. Essential for NGOs with foreign funding (FCRA scrutiny) or government grants.

### Q: Do NGO workers need workers' compensation?
**A:** Yes, if the NGO has 10+ employees. The Employees' Compensation Act 1923 applies to NGOs. Also consider ESI if employees earn below ₹21,000/month. Volunteer injuries are not covered by workers' comp — need separate volunteer accident policy.

### Q: Can NGOs claim insurance premium as expense?
**A:** Yes. All insurance premiums (PL, workers' comp, asset, D&O, cyber) are deductible under Section 37(1). NGOs registered under Section 12A may have additional tax exemptions. Consult your CA for proper treatment.

### Q: Do NGOs need cyber insurance?
**A:** Yes. NGOs store beneficiary data (Aadhaar, health records, financial info). DPDP Act 2023 applies to NGOs. One data breach can cost ₹5-25L in notification + legal + compensation. FCRA compliance also requires data protection for foreign-funded projects.
