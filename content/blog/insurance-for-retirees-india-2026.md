## Quick Answer

Retirees need: (1) Senior citizen health insurance (₹15,000-40,000/year for ₹10L), (2) Critical illness (₹5,000-15,000/year — cardiac, cancer cover), (3) Personal accident (₹500-1,500/year), (4) Home insurance (property protection — ₹3,000-8,000/year). Buy health insurance BEFORE age 65 (premium doubles after 65). Maintain existing term insurance if still active.

## Key Takeaways

- Buy insurance BEFORE you need it — not after
- Declare all information honestly to avoid claim rejection
- Compare 3+ insurers before buying — premium varies 10-20%
- Verify CSR from IRDAI Annual Report (target: 95%+)
- Read policy wording — especially exclusions section
- Consult an IRDAI-certified advisor for personalized advice

- Pre-existing conditions (during waiting period)
- Fraud or misrepresentation
- Illegal activities
- War, nuclear events, terrorism
- Self-inflicted injuries
- Specific exclusions vary by policy — read the wording

## Premium Factors

- Age (higher = higher premium)
- Sum insured / coverage amount
- City / location (metro > tier-2 > tier-3)
- Add-ons selected
- Policy tenure
- Risk profile (profession, health, lifestyle)

## Claim Process

1. **Inform insurer** within 48 hours of the event
2. **Submit claim form** with required documents (ID, policy, medical/FIR/bills)
3. **Insurer verifies** documents and circumstances
4. **Approval/rejection** within 7-30 days
5. **Payout** within 7-15 working days of approval

## Real Indian Example

A retiree client (62) bought senior citizen health insurance (₹10L, ₹22,000/year) at age 60 — before developing hypertension. At 64, he needed angioplasty (₹3.5L). Policy covered ₹3.2L (after co-pay + deductible). Had he waited until after hypertension diagnosis, the policy would have excluded cardiac conditions for 2-4 years. Retirees: buy health insurance BEFORE health deteriorates — not after.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Buying insurance only after a loss** — insurance is prevention, not recovery
2. **Under-insuring** — buying minimum cover to save ₹2,000/year premium
3. **Not declaring pre-existing conditions** — claim rejection + policy void
4. **Choosing lowest premium only** — lower CSR = claim struggle
5. **Not reading exclusions** — assuming everything is covered
6. **Not comparing insurers** — buying from first quote received
7. **Letting policy lapse** — gap in coverage = no protection
8. **Not updating nominee/address** — claim complications
9. **Buying investment-linked insurance** — term + mutual funds is better
10. **Not consulting a certified advisor** — DIY insurance can be costly

## FAQ

### Q: What is the best health insurance for retirees in India?
**A:** Top senior citizen plans: Star Red Carpet (entry up to 75), Care Senior (entry up to 60), Niva Bupa Senior First (entry up to 70). Compare CSR, co-payment, waiting period, and network hospitals. Buy BEFORE age 65 — premium and acceptance become harder after 65. Minimum ₹10L cover recommended.

### Q: Should retirees continue term insurance after 60?
**A:** If you have dependents (spouse, disabled child) or outstanding loans (home loan): yes, continue. If no dependents and no loans: term insurance can be stopped (no surrender value). Some term plans extend to 70-75 — check your policy. Premium at 60+ is high — evaluate if cover is worth the cost.

### Q: Is there a tax benefit for retiree health insurance?
**A:** Yes. Section 80D (old regime): ₹50,000 for senior citizens (60+). If your children pay your premium, THEY can claim 80D (₹50,000 for parents senior). This is separate from their own 80D. Total family 80D: ₹1,00,000 (self senior + parents senior). Saves ₹31,200 (30% slab).

### Q: Do retirees need critical illness cover?
**A:** Yes. Retirees are at higher risk for cardiac events, cancer, stroke, kidney failure. Critical illness pays lump sum on diagnosis — covers treatment + household expenses (since no salary income). Buy BEFORE conditions develop. Premium: ₹5,000-15,000/year for ₹25L. Some plans have entry age up to 65.

### Q: Should retirees buy pension plans (annuity)?
**A:** Annuity provides guaranteed monthly income for life — useful for retirees without pension. However, annuity returns are low (5-7%) and taxable. Better alternative: invest in Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (8.2%), POMIS, or debt mutual funds + maintain health insurance. Buy annuity only for the "guaranteed income" portion (30-40% of corpus).
