## Quick Answer

Students (18-25) should buy: (1) Health insurance (parents' floater may not cover after 25 — buy individual ₹5L for ₹3,000-5,000/year), (2) Personal accident (₹200-500/year for ₹10L — high accident risk for young drivers/riders), (3) Term insurance (optional — buy if dependents exist or to lock in low premium for life). Student-specific: travel insurance for study abroad, hostel belongings cover.

## FAQ

### Q: Am I covered under my parents' health insurance after 18?
**A:** Yes, typically until age 25 (some insurers extend to 28 if full-time student). After that, you need your own policy. Buy BEFORE age 25 to avoid coverage gap. If your parents' policy is family floater, you are automatically covered — check the age limit in policy wording.

### Q: Should students buy term insurance?
**A:** If you have no dependents: not urgent. However, buying at 22-25 locks in the LOWEST premium for life (₹4,000-6,000/year for ₹1Cr, locked for 40 years). At 30, same cover costs ₹10,000+. If you have education loans with co-borrower parents: buy term insurance for loan amount — protects parents from loan burden if you die.

### Q: Do students need personal accident insurance?
**A:** Yes. Students have high accident risk: bike riding, sports, adventure activities, college trips. PA cover (₹10L) costs ₹200-500/year — the cheapest insurance you will ever buy. Covers death, disability, and medical expenses from accidents. No medical test required.

### Q: What insurance do students studying abroad need?
**A:** 1) Travel insurance with study-abroad cover (medical + baggage + passport + trip cancellation), 2) Health insurance valid in destination country (Indian policy may not cover abroad), 3) Personal accident (valid worldwide), 4) Hostel/belongings insurance. Premium: ₹3,000-8,000/year for travel + health combo.

### Q: Can students claim 80D on health insurance?
**A:** Yes, if they pay premium from own income (internship, freelance). ₹25,000 deduction (under 60, old regime). If parents pay, THEY claim 80D (not the student). If student has no taxable income, 80D benefit is unused but insurance is still essential.
