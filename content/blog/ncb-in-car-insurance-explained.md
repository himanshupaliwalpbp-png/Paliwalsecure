## Key Takeaway

**No-Claim Bonus (NCB)** is a discount on your Own Damage (OD) premium — ranging from **20% to 50%** — awarded for every claim-free year. After 5 consecutive claim-free years, you get the maximum 50% discount. NCB applies only to the OD premium (not TP), stays with the policyholder (not the car), and can be transferred when you switch insurers or buy a new car. The **NCB Protector add-on** (₹800-2,000/year) preserves your NCB even after 1-2 claims. Never lose your NCB by making small claims — a ₹3,000 claim can cost you ₹5,000-8,000 in lost NCB over the next 5 years.

## Key Facts

- **NCB ranges from 20% to 50%** of OD premium over 5 claim-free years
- **NCB belongs to the policyholder**, not the car — it transfers when you sell your car
- **Even a single claim resets NCB to 0%** — unless you have NCB protector
- **NCB can save up to ₹15,000-25,000/year** on premium for expensive cars
- **NCB Protector add-on costs just ₹800-2,000/year** and preserves NCB after 1-2 claims

## What is No-Claim Bonus (NCB)?

No-Claim Bonus (NCB) — also called **Dabi Mukt Bhoonus** in Hindi — is a reward from the insurance company for not filing any claims during the policy year. It's a discount on your Own Damage (OD) premium that increases with each consecutive claim-free year.

### Key Principles of NCB

1. **NCB applies only to OD premium** — not to third-party premium or add-ons
2. **NCB is linked to the policyholder** — not the vehicle
3. **NCB accumulates over consecutive claim-free years**
4. **A single claim resets NCB to 0%** (unless you have NCB protector)
5. **NCB is transferable** between insurers and vehicles
6. **NCB has a 90-day validity** after policy expiry — renew in time to retain it

## How NCB Works: A Practical Timeline

### Year-by-Year NCB Accumulation

| Year | Claims Filed | NCB at Renewal | OD Premium (₹15,000 base) | Amount Paid |
|------|-------------|----------------|--------------------------|-------------|
| Year 1 | 0 | 0% (first year) | ₹15,000 | ₹15,000 |
| Year 2 | 0 | 20% | ₹12,000 | ₹12,000 |
| Year 3 | 0 | 25% | ₹11,250 | ₹11,250 |

| Year 5 | 0 | 45% | ₹8,250 | ₹8,250 |
| Year 6 | 0 | 50% | ₹7,500 | ₹7,500 |

### What Happens When You File a Claim?

| Year | Claims Filed | NCB at Renewal | Impact |
|------|-------------|----------------|--------|
| Year 1 | 0 | 20% | Normal progression |
| Year 2 | 0 | 25% | Normal progression |
| Year 3 | 1 claim | 0% | **NCB resets to zero!** |
| Year 4 | 0 | 20% | Starts accumulating again |
| Year 5 | 0 | 25% | Still not back to previous level |

**The cost of that one claim in Year 3: Lost NCB of ₹5,250 (35%) in Year 3 + ₹3,000 (difference between 35% and 20%) in Year 4 + ₹1,500 (difference between 25% and 35%) in Year 5 = ₹9,750 in lost NCB benefits.**

## NCB Protector Add-On

The **NCB Protector** (also called NCB Retention or NCB Preservation) is an add-on that protects your accumulated NCB even if you file a claim.

### How NCB Protector Works

| Without NCB Protector | With NCB Protector |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| 1 claim → NCB resets to 0% | 1-2 claims → NCB remains intact |
| 2 claims → NCB resets to 0% | After 2 claims → NCB drops by one slab |
| Must rebuild NCB from scratch | NCB protected for the current policy year |

### NCB Protector Premium

| Vehicle | OD Premium | NCB Protector Cost (10-15% of OD) |
|---------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Maruti Swift (₹8,000 OD) | ₹8,000 | ₹800-1,200/year |
| Hyundai Creta (₹15,000 OD) | ₹15,000 | ₹1,500-2,250/year |
| Toyota Fortuner (₹35,000 OD) | ₹35,000 | ₹3,500-5,250/year |

### Is NCB Protector Worth It?

**Yes, if:**
- You have accumulated 35%+ NCB (3+ claim-free years)
- Your OD premium is ₹10,000+ (NCB savings exceed protector cost)
- You live in a city with high accident/traffic risk
- You want peace of mind

**No, if:**
- You have 0-20% NCB (not much to protect)
- Your OD premium is very low (₹3,000-5,000)
- You rarely drive or have very low accident risk

## Transferring NCB When Selling Your Car

One of the most important things to know: **NCB belongs to you, not the car.** When you sell your car, you can transfer the NCB to your new car.

### Step-by-Step NCB Transfer Process

1. **Sell your old car** and get the delivery note/sale agreement
2. **Request NCB certificate** from your current insurer
3. **Submit sale proof** (delivery note, RC transfer acknowledgment)
4. **Insurer issues NCB certificate** within 7-15 days
5. **Present NCB certificate** when buying insurance for your new car
6. **New insurer honors the NCB** — same discount on OD premium

### NCB Certificate

An NCB certificate is an official document from your insurer stating your NCB percentage. It's valid for **3 years** from the date of issue.

**Documents required for NCB certificate:**
- Policy document of old car
- Sale agreement / delivery note
- RC transfer proof (Form 29/30)
- Request letter to insurer

### Common Mistakes During Car Sale

| Mistake | Consequence | Correct Approach |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| Not taking NCB certificate | Lose accumulated NCB (worth ₹5,000-17,000/year) | Always request NCB certificate before cancelling policy |
| Transferring policy to buyer with NCB | Buyer gets YOUR NCB benefit | Retain NCB, transfer policy without NCB discount |
| Cancelling policy without NCB certificate | NCB is lost permanently | Get NCB certificate FIRST, then cancel |
| Not using NCB within 3 years | NCB certificate expires | Apply NCB to new car insurance within validity |

## NCB When Switching Insurers

You can transfer your NCB when switching from one insurer to another at renewal:

### Process
1. Get a renewal quote from the new insurer (with your current NCB)
2. Declare your current NCB percentage
3. The new insurer verifies with the previous insurer through IRDAI's NCB database
4. Your NCB is applied to the new policy

**Important:** IRDAI has digitized NCB records. Insurers can verify your NCB online — so don't exaggerate your NCB percentage. The new insurer will check.

### Documents Required
- Previous year's policy document
- Renewal notice showing NCB percentage
- No-claim declaration (if asked)

## NCB and Zero Depreciation: How They Interact

A common confusion: Does making a zero dep claim affect NCB?

**Yes, it does.** Any claim — whether with zero dep or without — counts as a claim and resets your NCB to 0%. Zero dep only ensures you don't pay depreciation on parts; it doesn't protect your NCB.

### The Only Exception: NCB Protector
If you have both zero dep and NCB protector add-ons:
- Zero dep: No depreciation on parts replacement
- NCB protector: NCB stays intact after 1-2 claims

**This is the "platinum protection" combo** — recommended for cars with high NCB and high OD premium.

## NCB in Different Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Car Meets with an Accident (Your Fault)
- File claim → NCB resets to 0%
- Don't file → Pay from pocket, NCB preserved
- With NCB protector → File claim, NCB preserved

### Scenario 2: Car is Stolen
- File theft claim → NCB resets to 0% (yes, even theft counts as a claim!)
- Use NCB certificate from previous policy for your new car's insurance

### Scenario 3: Windshield Replacement
- Small crack → File claim → NCB resets
- Cost of windshield: ₹5,000-15,000
- NCB loss: ₹5,000-17,500/year for next 5 years
- Usually better to pay from pocket

### Scenario 4: Someone Hits Your Parked Car
- File claim with YOUR insurer → NCB resets
- File claim with OTHER party's insurer (third-party claim) → Your NCB is NOT affected
- **Always try to claim from the at-fault party's insurance** when possible

### Scenario 5: Car is Damaged in a Flood
- File claim → NCB resets
- Engine protect covers engine damage but claim still resets NCB
- Unless you have NCB protector, your NCB is lost

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Does NCB apply to third-party premium?
No. NCB is a discount only on the Own Damage (OD) premium. Third-party premium is fixed by IRDAI and cannot be discounted. The TP portion of your premium remains the same regardless of NCB.

### 2. Can I transfer NCB from my old car to my new bike?
No. NCB transfer is allowed only between the same vehicle type (car to car, bike to bike). You cannot transfer car NCB to a two-wheeler policy or vice versa.

### 3. What happens to NCB if I don't renew on time?
NCB is valid for 90 days after policy expiry. If you renew within 90 days, your NCB is preserved. After 90 days, NCB is lost permanently. Always renew on time!

### 4. Does NCB apply to add-on premiums?
No. NCB discount applies only to the base OD premium. Add-on premiums (zero dep, engine protect, NCB protector, etc.) are charged at full rate regardless of your NCB.

### 5. Can I carry forward NCB from company car to personal car?
Generally, NCB from a company-owned car (commercial insurance) cannot be transferred to a personal car (private insurance). However, if the car is registered in your name and has personal insurance, the NCB can be transferred.

### 6. Is there any way to get NCB without claim-free years?
No. NCB is earned exclusively through claim-free years. No insurer offers NCB without meeting the claim-free requirement. Be wary of anyone claiming otherwise.

### 7. Does a rejected claim affect NCB?
No. If you file a claim that is rejected (not settled by the insurer), it typically does not count against your NCB. Only settled claims reset NCB. However, verify this with your specific insurer.

### 8. Can I get NCB on a new car (first insurance)?
No. NCB is not available on a brand-new car's first insurance. NCB starts accumulating from the first renewal (after one claim-free year). At first renewal, you get 20% NCB.
