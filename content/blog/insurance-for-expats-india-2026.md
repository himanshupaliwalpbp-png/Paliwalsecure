## Quick Answer

Expats living in India need: health insurance (Indian policy or international plan with India coverage), life insurance (available if resident for 182+ days), motor insurance (mandatory for any vehicle), and renter's insurance. Most Indian insurers offer policies to expats with valid visa + FRRO registration. Premiums may be 10-20% higher than Indian citizens.

## Claim Process

1. **Inform insurer** within 48 hours of the event
2. **Submit claim form** with required documents (ID, policy, medical/FIR/bills)
3. **Insurer verifies** documents and circumstances
4. **Approval/rejection** within 7-30 days
5. **Payout** within 7-15 working days of approval

## Common Mistakes

1. **Buying insurance only after a loss** — insurance is prevention, not recovery
2. **Under-insuring** — buying minimum cover to save ₹2,000/year premium
3. **Not declaring pre-existing conditions** — claim rejection + policy void
4. **Choosing lowest premium only** — lower CSR = claim struggle
5. **Not reading exclusions** — assuming everything is covered
6. **Not comparing insurers** — buying from first quote received
7. **Letting policy lapse** — gap in coverage = no protection
8. **Not updating nominee/address** — claim complications
9. **Buying investment-linked insurance** — term + mutual funds is better
10. **Not consulting a certified advisor** — DIY insurance can be costly

## FAQ

### Q: Can expats buy health insurance in India?
**A:** Yes. Most Indian insurers offer health insurance to expats with valid employment/business visa + FRRO registration. You need: passport, visa, FRRO certificate, address proof, and sometimes a local bank account. Premiums may be 10-20% higher than Indian citizens due to higher medical costs for international patients.

### Q: Should expats buy Indian health insurance or international?
**A:** If you plan to stay 2+ years in India: buy Indian policy (cheaper, cashless at Indian hospitals, 80D not applicable but lower premium). If you travel frequently: international plan with India coverage (covers treatment in home country too). Best option: buy Indian policy for daily coverage + maintain home country policy for visits.

### Q: Can expats buy life insurance in India?
**A:** Yes, if resident in India for 182+ days in the financial year (resident status per Income Tax Act). Need: passport, visa, FRRO registration, income proof, and sometimes medical examination. NRIs and OCIs can also buy Indian life insurance. Term insurance is available from most major insurers.

### Q: Is motor insurance mandatory for expats in India?
**A:** Yes. Motor Vehicles Act applies to all vehicles regardless of owner nationality. Third-party insurance is mandatory. Expats can buy comprehensive motor insurance with valid Indian driving license or international driving permit (IDP). Premium is same as Indian citizens.

### Q: What insurance should expats buy when renting in India?
**A:** Renter's contents insurance: covers laptop, furniture, electronics against theft, fire, flood. Premium: ₹1,500-3,000/year for ₹5L cover. Also verify health insurance network hospitals near your residence — metro cities have better network coverage than tier-2 cities.
