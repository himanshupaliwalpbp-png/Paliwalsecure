## Quick Answer

Single women should prioritize: (1) Health insurance (₹10L+ cover, ₹5,000-10,000/year), (2) Term life insurance (income replacement if dependents exist — ₹3,000-8,000/year), (3) Critical illness cover (women-specific: breast cancer, cervical cancer — ₹3,000-8,000/year), (4) Personal accident (₹500-1,500/year). Women get 10-15% lower term insurance premiums than men (longer life expectancy).

- Risk profile (profession, health, lifestyle)

## Claim Process

1. **Inform insurer** within 48 hours of the event
2. **Submit claim form** with required documents (ID, policy, medical/FIR/bills)
3. **Insurer verifies** documents and circumstances
4. **Approval/rejection** within 7-30 days
5. **Payout** within 7-15 working days of approval

## Common Mistakes

1. **Buying insurance only after a loss** — insurance is prevention, not recovery
2. **Under-insuring** — buying minimum cover to save ₹2,000/year premium
3. **Not declaring pre-existing conditions** — claim rejection + policy void
4. **Choosing lowest premium only** — lower CSR = claim struggle
5. **Not reading exclusions** — assuming everything is covered
6. **Not comparing insurers** — buying from first quote received
7. **Letting policy lapse** — gap in coverage = no protection
8. **Not updating nominee/address** — claim complications
9. **Buying investment-linked insurance** — term + mutual funds is better
10. **Not consulting a certified advisor** — DIY insurance can be costly

## FAQ

### Q: Do single women need life insurance if they have no dependents?
**A:** If truly no dependents (no parents, no children, no siblings depending on you): term insurance is lower priority. However, if you support aging parents or may have dependents in future: buy term insurance NOW (lowest premium at young age, locked for 30+ years). Premium at 25: ₹8,000/year for ₹1Cr. At 35: ₹15,000+. Buy early.

### Q: Is term insurance cheaper for women?
**A:** Yes. Women typically pay 10-15% less than men for the same term insurance (based on longer life expectancy). Some insurers offer "women-specific" plans with additional riders (critical illness, maternity). Always compare quotes — the gender discount is automatic, you don't need to ask for it.

### Q: Should single women buy critical illness cover?
**A:** Yes. Women are at higher risk for: breast cancer (1 in 28 Indian women), cervical cancer, ovarian cancer. Critical illness pays a lump sum on diagnosis — covers treatment + lost income + lifestyle adjustment. Women-specific CI plans cover breast/cervical cancer from day one (no waiting period). Premium: ₹3,000-8,000/year for ₹25L.

### Q: What is the ideal insurance portfolio for a single woman (30, ₹12L income)?
**A:** 1) Health insurance: ₹10L individual cover (₹6,000/year). 2) Term insurance: ₹1Cr if supporting parents (₹8,000/year). 3) Critical illness: ₹25L women-specific (₹5,000/year). 4) Personal accident: ₹10L (₹500/year). Total: ₹19,500/year for ₹1.45Cr+ total protection. 80D saves ₹1,860; 80C saves ₹2,480 (old regime).

### Q: Can single women claim 80D for parents' health insurance?
**A:** Yes. If you pay premium for parents' health insurance, you can claim additional 80D deduction: ₹25,000 (parents under 60) or ₹50,000 (parents 60+). This is IN ADDITION to your own 80D deduction. Total: ₹50,000 (self) + ₹50,000 (parents) = ₹1,00,000 maximum. Saves ₹31,200/year (30% slab).
