## What is Health Insurance?

Health insurance is a contract between you and an insurance company where the insurer agrees to pay for your medical expenses — hospitalization, surgeries, day-care procedures, pre and post-hospitalization costs — in exchange for a regular premium. In India, health insurance operates under the regulatory framework of IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India).

Under **Section 80D** of the Income Tax Act, you can claim a deduction of up to ₹25,000 per year for health insurance premiums paid for yourself, your spouse, and dependent children. An additional ₹25,000 is available for insuring parents (₹50,000 if parents are senior citizens). With GST now exempt on health insurance, the effective cost has reduced by 18% starting September 2025.

## How to Choose the Best Health Insurance Plan

### Step 1: Determine Your Coverage Need

Calculate your ideal sum insured based on:
- **City of residence:** Metro cities need ₹15-25 lakh; Tier-2/3 cities need ₹10-15 lakh
- **Family size:** Individual vs. family floater — a family of 4 needs at least ₹15-20 lakh
- **Medical history:** If you have pre-existing conditions, factor in potential higher costs
- **Age:** Premiums increase with age — buy early to lock in lower rates

### Step 2: Compare Key Metrics

Focus on these four metrics when comparing plans:
1. **Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR):** Higher is better — aim for 95%+
2. **Network Hospitals:** More hospitals = easier cashless claims
3. **Room Rent Limit:** "No limit" is ideal; capped room rent can cause partial claim rejections
4. **Restoration Benefit:** Unlimited restoration > 100% once > no restoration

### Step 3: Check Waiting Periods

- **Initial waiting period:** 30 days (standard across all insurers)
- **PED waiting period:** 2-4 years (shorter is better)
- **Specific treatment:** 1-2 years for cataract, hernia, joint replacement
- **Maternity:** 9-36 months (if applicable)

### Step 4: Evaluate Add-Ons

Essential add-ons to consider:
- **Super Top-Up:** Adds ₹15-25 lakh coverage for just ₹3,000-5,000/year
- **Maternity Cover:** Important for planning families
- **Room Rent Waiver:** Removes room rent capping
- **Global Coverage:** For international treatment options

### Step 5: Check the Fine Print

Before purchasing, read the policy wording for:
- Co-payment clauses (common in senior citizen plans)
- Sub-limits on specific treatments
- Exclusions list (cosmetic surgery, self-inflicted injury, etc.)
- Pre-authorization requirements for cashless claims

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Buying Only ₹5 Lakh Coverage
A single hospitalization for a critical illness can cost ₹10-20 lakh in a metro hospital. Always buy at least ₹10 lakh coverage, and add a super top-up for extra protection.

### 2. Not Disclosing Pre-Existing Conditions
Hiding diabetes, hypertension, or thyroid issues during application is the #1 reason for claim rejection. Always disclose all conditions honestly.

### 3. Choosing the Cheapest Plan Without Checking CSR
A ₹2,000/year savings on premium means nothing if your claim is rejected. Always prioritize claim settlement ratio over premium.

### 4. Ignoring Room Rent Limits
Many plans cap room rent at 1% of sum insured. If your room costs more, the insurer applies a proportional deduction on the entire bill — not just the room charges. This can reduce your claim by 30-40%.

### 5. Not Adding Super Top-Up
A super top-up plan costing just ₹3,000-5,000/year adds ₹15-25 lakh of additional coverage. It activates only when your base plan is exhausted, making it incredibly cost-effective.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. How much health insurance do I need?
Experts recommend coverage of at least 50% of your annual income, with a minimum of ₹10 lakh. For families in metro cities, ₹15-25 lakh is recommended due to higher medical costs.

### 2. Is health insurance premium tax-deductible?
Yes, under Section 80D of the Income Tax Act, you can deduct up to ₹25,000 per year for yourself and family, plus an additional ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizen parents) for parents' health insurance.

### 3. What is cashless hospitalization?
Cashless hospitalization allows you to get treated at a network hospital without paying upfront. The insurer settles the bill directly with the hospital. Over 10,000 hospitals across India offer this facility.

### 4. Can I switch my health insurance provider?
Yes, IRDAI allows portability. Apply at least 45 days before your renewal date. Your accumulated benefits (waiting period credits, NCB) transfer to the new insurer.

### 5. What is a super top-up plan?
A super top-up plan provides additional coverage above your base health insurance. It activates when your total medical bills in a year exceed the deductible (usually ₹5-10 lakh). It costs ₹3,000-5,000/year for ₹15-25 lakh extra coverage.

### 6. Are pre-existing diseases covered?
Yes, but after a waiting period of 2-4 years depending on the insurer. Niva Bupa and ACKO offer the shortest PED waiting periods at 2 years on select plans.

### 7. What is No-Claim Bonus (NCB)?
NCB increases your sum insured for every claim-free year. Typically 10% per year up to 50-100%. Care Health offers the highest NCB in the market — up to 150%.

### 8. Is GST still applicable on health insurance?
No. As of 22 September 2025, GST on individual and family floater health insurance policies has been exempted per the GST Council's 56th Meeting decision. This makes health insurance 18% cheaper.
