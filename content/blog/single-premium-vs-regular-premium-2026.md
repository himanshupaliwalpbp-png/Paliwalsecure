## Quick Verdict

For most Indian consumers, regular premium is better than single premium. Regular premium offers flexibility, lower upfront cost, and the ability to switch insurers. Single premium makes sense only if you have a lump sum amount, want to avoid yearly payments, and are confident about the policy for its entire term.

|---|---|---|

## What is Single Premium (One-time)?

You pay the entire premium for the policy term in one lump sum at the start. Common in term insurance, ULIPs, and health insurance (for multi-year policies).

## What is Regular Premium (Annual/Monthly)?

You pay the premium annually, semi-annually, quarterly, or monthly throughout the policy term. This is the default for most insurance policies.

## Feature Comparison Table

| Feature | Single Premium (One-time) | Regular Premium (Annual/Monthly) |
|---|---|---|
| Payment frequency | One-time lump sum | Annual, semi-annual, quarterly, or monthly |
| Upfront cost | High (full term premium) | Low (one year's premium) |
| Total premium paid | Lower (discount for upfront payment) | Higher (spread over years) |
| Flexibility to switch | Low — locked in for full term | High — can switch at renewal |
| Lapse risk | Zero (already paid) | Moderate — must remember to renew |
| Refund on cancellation | Limited (surrender value) | Pro-rated refund possible |
| Best for | Lump sum available, want simplicity | Salary earners, want flexibility |
| Tax benefit timing | One-time (year of payment) | Spread across multiple years |

## Who Should Choose Single Premium (One-time)?

You have a lump sum amount (e.g., bonus, maturity proceeds) and want to pay once and forget. Single premium also suits NRIs who find it difficult to make yearly payments from abroad.

## Who Should Choose Regular Premium (Annual/Monthly)?

You want to keep your money invested elsewhere (mutual funds, FDs) and pay insurance premium yearly. Regular premium also gives you the flexibility to switch insurers or adjust coverage as your needs change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is single premium cheaper than regular premium?
**A:** Yes, the total amount paid is lower with single premium (typically 20-30% less). However, the opportunity cost of locking in a lump sum can be higher than the savings, especially if you can invest that money at 10%+ returns.

### Q: Can I claim 80D tax benefit on single premium health insurance?
**A:** Yes, but only up to the annual limit (₹25,000 for self, ₹50,000 for senior parents). If your single premium exceeds the annual limit, the excess cannot be carried forward. With regular premium, you claim the benefit every year.

### Q: What happens if I cancel a single premium policy?
**A:** You get a surrender value, which is usually much lower than what you paid. The surrender value depends on the policy type and how long you've held it. Read the policy document for exact surrender terms.

### Q: Can I switch from single premium to regular premium?
**A:** No. Once you choose single premium, you cannot switch to regular premium for the same policy. You would need to cancel (with surrender loss) and buy a new policy.

### Q: Do single premium policies have a free-look period?
**A:** Yes. IRDAI mandates a 15-day free-look period for all insurance policies, including single premium. If you cancel within 15 days, you get a full refund (minus proportional risk charge and stamp duty).
